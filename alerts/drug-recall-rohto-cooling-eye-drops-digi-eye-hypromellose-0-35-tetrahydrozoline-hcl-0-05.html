Rohto Cooling Eye Drops Recall: Sterility Concerns Trigger Nationwide FDA Alert

Published: 2026-07-14  |  Severity: High  |  Source: Original Publication

Article Analysis & Summary

Featured Article on Zero483: The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has announced a Class II recall for Rohto Cooling Eye Drops Digi Eye, a popular over-the-counter ophthalmic solution. The recall, initiated by Rohto-Mentholatum (Vietnam) Co., Ltd., impacts 65,219 cartons distributed nationwide across the United States. The critical reason for this extensive recall is a "lack of assurance of sterility," raising significant concerns about the safety and efficacy of the product.

Ophthalmic products like eye drops are categorized as sterile medications, meaning they must be entirely free from all microorganisms – bacteria, viruses, fungi, and spores. This stringent requirement is non-negotiable because the eye is a highly sensitive and vulnerable organ. Any breach in sterility for a product designed for direct application to the eye can introduce harmful pathogens, potentially leading to severe eye infections, inflammation, vision impairment, or even permanent blindness if left untreated. The ingredients in question, hypromellose (a lubricating agent often found in artificial tears) and tetrahydrozoline HCl (a vasoconstrictor used to reduce eye redness), are intended to provide comfort and relief, but their benefits are overshadowed by the risks associated with a non-sterile formulation.

The specific product affected is Rohto Cooling Eye Drops Digi Eye, containing hypromellose 0.35% and tetrahydrozoline HCl 0.05%. It is sold in 0.4 FL OZ (13 mL) sterile bottles, bearing UPC 310742010602 and NDC 10742-8175-1. The product is manufactured in Vietnam and distributed by The Mentholatum Company, located in Orchard Park, NY. The Class II classification signifies that exposure to the product may cause temporary or reversible adverse health consequences, or that the probability of serious adverse health consequences is remote. However, for a sterile product, this risk is taken extremely seriously due to the delicate nature of the eye.

This ongoing recall emphasizes the critical importance of robust manufacturing controls and quality assurance throughout the supply chain, especially for products intended for direct application to sensitive areas of the body. For consumers, it underscores the need to remain vigilant about the products they use and to be aware of official health alerts. The FDA’s swift action aims to protect public health by removing potentially contaminated products from the market and alerting both consumers and healthcare professionals to the potential risks involved.

Context & Background

The market for over-the-counter eye drops is a colossal industry, fueled by increasing screen time, environmental allergens, and an aging population. Products like Rohto Cooling Eye Drops Digi Eye cater to common complaints such as dry eyes, redness, and irritation. Hypromellose, also known as hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (HPMC), is a widely used ingredient in artificial tears. It acts as a lubricant, forming a protective film over the eye to relieve dryness and discomfort. Tetrahydrozoline HCl, on the other hand, is a common decongestant that works by constricting blood vessels in the eye, reducing redness and making eyes appear whiter. These ingredients, when formulated correctly and under sterile conditions, provide welcomed relief to millions.

However, the efficacy and safety of ophthalmic products are entirely dependent on their sterility. The eye's natural defenses, while present, are not impenetrable. The cornea and conjunctiva are highly susceptible to microbial invasion if contaminants are introduced directly. Contaminated eye drops can lead to a range of severe conditions, including bacterial keratitis (an infection of the cornea), conjunctivitis, or even endophthalmitis (an infection inside the eye). These infections can rapidly progress, causing pain, inflammation, scarring, and potentially irreversible vision loss. This is precisely why the manufacturing of ophthalmic solutions requires meticulously controlled environments, aseptic processing techniques, and rigorous sterilization protocols to prevent any microbial contamination.

Rohto is a globally recognized brand in eye care, known for its distinctive cooling sensation products. A recall of this nature, even with a Class II classification, carries significant weight not just for the specific manufacturer, Rohto-Mentholatum (Vietnam) Co., Ltd., but for the broader pharmaceutical and consumer health industries. It highlights the FDA’s unwavering commitment to enforcing quality and safety standards for all products imported into the U.S., regardless of their country of origin. This incident serves as a stark reminder that consumer trust is built on consistent quality and adherence to the highest manufacturing standards, particularly for sterile products where the margin for error is virtually nonexistent.

Key Implications & Public Impact

  • Patient Safety Risk: The primary concern is the potential for serious eye infections, which could lead to pain, inflammation, blurred vision, and in severe cases, permanent vision impairment or blindness if contaminated eye drops are used.
  • Erosion of Consumer Trust: Recalls of popular brands can significantly damage consumer confidence in over-the-counter eye care products and the affected brand, leading to skepticism about product safety and quality.
  • Intensified Regulatory Scrutiny: This recall will likely lead to increased scrutiny from the FDA on foreign manufacturing facilities, particularly those producing sterile products, to ensure strict adherence to Current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP) and quality control standards.
  • Market Disruption and Financial Impact: Retailers and distributors face the logistical challenge and financial burden of identifying, removing, and returning affected products, potentially impacting sales and inventory management.
  • Brand Reputation Damage: Despite being a Class II recall, the "lack of assurance of sterility" issue can significantly tarnish Rohto's global reputation, potentially impacting future sales and market share as consumers may opt for alternative brands.
Key Takeaways & Recommended Actions:
  • For Consumers: Immediately check your medicine cabinet for Rohto Cooling Eye Drops Digi Eye (hypromellose 0.35%, tetrahydrozoline HCl 0.05%). If you possess the recalled product, discontinue use immediately and dispose of it safely. If you have used the product and experience any eye irritation, redness, discharge, pain, or changes in vision, consult a healthcare professional without delay.
  • For Retailers and Distributors: Promptly quarantine and cease distribution of all affected Rohto Cooling Eye Drops Digi Eye products. Follow the recall instructions provided by Rohto-Mentholatum (Vietnam) Co., Ltd. or their U.S. distributor, The Mentholatum Company, for returning or destroying the recalled inventory.
  • For Healthcare Professionals: Be aware of this recall when advising patients. Counsel patients who present with eye irritation or infection to check if they have used the recalled eye drops and to provide this information during their examination.
  • For All Pharmaceutical Manufacturers: This recall serves as a critical reminder of the paramount importance of maintaining robust Quality Management Systems and rigorous cGMP compliance, especially for sterile products. Regularly review and strengthen your manufacturing processes, sterility assurance protocols, and supply chain controls to prevent similar incidents.
