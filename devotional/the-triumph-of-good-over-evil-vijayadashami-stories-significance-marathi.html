The Origin & Sacred Meaning of Dussehra

Ashvin Shukla Paksha Dashami

Vijayadashami, also celebrated as Dussehra or Dasara, is one of the most spiritually vibrant festivals in the Hindu calendar. Celebrated on the tenth day of the waxing moon (Shukla Paksha) in the autumn month of Ashvin, it marks the triumphant culmination of the nine-day Navratri festival.

The word "Dussehra" is derived from Sanskrit: Dasha means ten, and Hara means defeat or removal. While traditions vary beautifully across India—from the giant effigies of Ravan Dahan in the North, to the majestic royal processions of Mysore Dasara in Karnataka, to the immersive Durga Puja celebrations in Bengal—the central message is universal: the eternal victory of Dharma over Adharma.

🏹

1. Lord Rama’s Victory Over Ravana (The Ramayana)

The Fall of Arrogance & Triumph of Righteousness

The Ramayana narrates the climactic battle where Lord Rama defeated the ten-headed king of Lanka, Ravana. Despite Ravana’s profound Vedic scholarship, immense penance, and golden kingdom, his ego and unchecked desires brought about his downfall.

With the devotion of Lakshmana, Hanuman, and the vanara army, Lord Rama invoked the blessings of Mother Durga through Akalbodhan and vanquished Ravana on Vijayadashami. This stands as an eternal reminder: intellect and power without humility and dharma inevitably lead to ruin.

🔥 The Living Tradition of Ravan Dahan:
Towering effigies of Ravana, Kumbhakarna, and Meghnad stuffed with firecrackers are set ablaze each Dussehra evening, symbolizing the public eradication of negative forces by the light of truth.

2. Goddess Durga’s Triumph Over Mahishasura

Devi Mahatmyam • Supreme Feminine Shakti

The Devi Mahatmyam recounts how the buffalo-demon Mahishasura, armed with a boon that no man could slay him, terrorized the cosmos. The Holy Trinity and celestial gods pooled their radiant spiritual energies (Tejas) to manifest the supreme feminine energy: Goddess Mahishasuramardini (Durga).

Riding a fierce lion and wielding divine weapons, Mother Durga engaged Mahishasura in fierce combat for nine days and nights, striking him down on the tenth day—Vijayadashami—emancipating all beings from fear and tyranny.

3. The Pandavas Reclaiming Their Weapons (The Mahabharata)

The Sacred Shami Tree & Ayudha Pooja

During their 13th year of exile incognito (Agyatvas), the five Pandava brothers safely concealed their divine weapons in the hollow branches of a sacred Shami tree near Virata Nagar.

On the day of Vijayadashami, when the Kauravas attacked Virata, Arjuna retrieved his celestial Gandiva bow and vanquished the Kaurava forces, safeguarding righteousness.

🌿 The Tradition of Exchanging Shami Leaves:
In Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Telangana, devotees visit Shami trees on Dussehra evening and exchange golden Shami leaves (Banni / Sonar Paan) with elders and loved ones as tokens of good fortune, peace, and mutual prosperity.

❓ विजयादशमी व दसऱ्याविषयी विचारले जाणारे प्रश्न (FAQs)

What is the exact Sanskrit meaning of 'Dussehra'?
'Dussehra' is formed from two Sanskrit roots: Dasha (meaning ten) and Hara (meaning defeat or destruction). It commemorates the destruction of the ten heads of Ravana, each signifying a human flaw.
Why did Lord Rama worship Goddess Durga before defeating Ravana?
Lord Rama performed an untimely invocation (Akalbodhan) of Goddess Durga during autumn. Pleased with Rama's intense devotion, Mother Durga blessed Him with the celestial power and wisdom needed to slay Ravana.
Why did Goddess Durga fight Mahishasura for nine nights?
The nine nights (Navratri) represent the progressive purification and conquest over every disguise of ego and delusion, culminating in total liberation on the tenth day (Vijayadashami).
Why do people perform Ayudha Pooja or Vahana Pooja on Vijayadashami?
Inspired by the Pandavas retrieving and sanctifying their weapons from the Shami tree before starting a victorious battle, people across India worship their tools of livelihood, books, vehicles, and instruments.
